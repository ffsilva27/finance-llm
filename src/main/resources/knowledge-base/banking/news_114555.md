But scientists in Europe have made headway in devising a blood and urine test that could one day help with earlier detection of the condition. Read: Noise Pollution May Impact Heart Health, According to New Research The researchers’ findings, published in the Molecular Autism Journal , showed chemical differences in the blood and urine of those studied with autism versus test subjects without the condition. In particular, they found higher levels of protein damage, especially in the blood plasma in the children with autism. Lead author of the study, Naila Rabbani, a biologist at the University of Warwick in the U.K., told Gizmodo that the test could improve the accuracy of diagnosis from 60-70% to up to 90% accuracy.
